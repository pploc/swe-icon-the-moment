## Question

Tell me about a time you strongly disagreed with a teammate or your manager
on a technical decision. What was the disagreement, what did you do, and how
did it end?

## Answer

**What the interviewer is scoring** — not who was right, but: do you
disagree with *evidence* instead of volume, can you commit to a decision that
went against you, and did you protect the relationship while contesting the
idea?

**Shape of a strong answer (STAR, ~3 minutes):**

- **Situation/Task** — one crisp paragraph with real stakes: "We had to pick
  between extending the monolith or a new service for X; my tech lead wanted
  the service, I believed we'd pay a year of operational tax for no benefit
  at our scale."
- **Action** — the substance. Strong signals: you sought *their* reasoning
  first and steel-manned it; you brought evidence, not opinion (a spike, load
  numbers, an incident count, a one-page doc); you kept it in the open
  (design review, ADR) rather than lobbying in DMs; you proposed a
  decision mechanism — time-boxed prototype, explicit success criteria,
  or escalation to a clearly-named decider.
- **Result** — either outcome works. If you prevailed: what shipped, what
  the numbers did, how you kept the other person whole. If you lost:
  **disagree-and-commit told honestly** — you executed their plan *well*,
  and what you learned (sometimes: they were right).

**Red flags to avoid:** a story where the other person was simply an idiot,
"I escalated" as the first move, winning by attrition, a "disagreement"
that's really a story about you being right, or commitment so weak you
quietly waited for the decision to fail.

## Follow-ups

- What would have made you change your mind?
- Tell me about a time you were *wrong* in one of these disputes.
- The decision went against you and the project is now visibly failing — what do you do?
